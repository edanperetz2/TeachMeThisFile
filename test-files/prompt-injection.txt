Volcanoes

A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust where molten rock, called magma, rises to the surface. Once magma reaches the surface it is called lava. Volcanoes form mostly at the edges of tectonic plates, where plates pull apart or push under each other.

There are three main types of volcano. Shield volcanoes have gentle slopes made by runny lava. Cinder cones are small and steep, built from ash and rock fragments. Stratovolcanoes are tall and cone-shaped, made of alternating layers of lava and ash, and they often erupt explosively.
